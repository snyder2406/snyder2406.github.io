Zero Trust Container Architecture (ZTCA)

MCPA · In Review · 2026

From Framework to Toolchain: Implementing Zero Trust Architecture in Cloud-Native Environments for DoW Compliance. Manuscript in review with the Military Cyber Professionals Association. Presented at the 2026 VICEROY Symposium, where it placed 1st in the student poster competition, judged by Department of War leadership and senior government officials.

THE
PROBLEM

NIST left the toolchain unspecified

NIST SP 800-207A defines a multi-tier Zero Trust policy model for cloud-native environments, but it's deliberately architecture-neutral — it never says which concrete Kubernetes tools should fill each role. That gap leaves practitioners guessing at implementation, and most existing Zero Trust research focuses on proposed architectures rather than validated deployments.

THE
FRAMEWORK

Mapping NIST's model onto real tools, across three tiers

ZTCA maps NIST's three logical components — Policy Engine, Policy Administrator, Policy Enforcement Point — onto a concrete Kubernetes toolchain at three enforcement tiers:

  • Tier 1 — Service mesh (L7 identity): Istio/Linkerd, SPIFFE/SPIRE workload identity, and Open Policy Agent enforcing per-request, identity-based authorization instead of per-session.
  • Tier 2 — Microsegmentation (L3/L4 network): Kubernetes NetworkPolicy enforced via Cilium or Calico, providing a defense-in-depth backup layer using eBPF.
  • Tier 3 — Perimeter: traditional firewalls, load balancers, and API gateways handling north-south traffic at the cluster boundary.

The key architectural shift: replacing IP-based policy with cryptographic SPIFFE workload identity as the primary trust anchor, since IP addresses are meaningless for ephemeral, dynamically-addressed containers.

VENDOR
ANALYSIS

No single vendor covers all three tiers

A structured comparison of six major Zero Trust vendors — Microsoft, Google BeyondCorp, Zscaler, Okta, Illumio, and HashiCorp — against NIST component alignment, Kubernetes-native capability, FedRAMP authorization posture, and empirical ROI. The finding: DoW deployments need a layered, multi-vendor approach, since no existing product fully instantiates the ZTCA model on its own.

FEDERAL
CONTEXT

Why this matters for DoW timelines

The Department of War has set a target of enterprise-wide Zero Trust deployment by FY2027. Federal progress data shows real movement — hardware asset coverage improving from 33% to 55%, software from 18% to 39% since the FY2022 baseline — but 61% of agencies remain below 90% software coverage. That gap between mandate and toolchain- level guidance is exactly what this research targets.

WHAT'S
NEXT

Stage 2: empirical validation

This is Stage 1 — an applied analysis and framework. A planned Stage 2 pilot will deploy the proposed toolchain in a controlled lab environment to empirically test whether the three-tier mapping holds up, whether audit coverage spans all three tiers, and whether the recommended multi-vendor stack actually interoperates.

Embedded Systems Compliance Research

AFRL / Griffiss Institute · VICEROY MAVEN · 2026

As part of a 7-person, 5-university research team at the Air Force Research Laboratory's Griffiss Institute, I co-developed the first government-initiated Security Technical Implementation Guide (STIG) for a custom embedded military operating system — a purpose-built system that doesn't expose the standard Unix primitives (shell, SSH, writable filesystem) that most federal compliance frameworks assume exist.

The work involved analyzing NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 controls and CIS Benchmark configurations, identifying which control intent could still be satisfied through the system's alternative management interface, and authoring 26 XCCDF rules across six control families — reflecting how the compliance boundary shifts when a system's architecture doesn't match conventional assumptions.

Because this system and its documentation are government-sensitive and not yet publicly releasable, I'm keeping the details general here. I'm glad to speak to the work, methodology, and my specific contributions in more depth in conversation.
